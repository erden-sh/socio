Spaces organize the workspace's pages. Open a Space to see its library, search for a document and switch between the available grid and list views. Open a page to read it, or create one for a piece of knowledge the team should keep.

## Shared documents, personal conversations

Spaces and their pages are shared material: with the whole workspace, or, in a private Space, with its people. People who can see a Space can edit its pages. Creating a page in a Space is a publishing decision, even if the notes came from a personal socio. Read [privacy and sharing](/docs/privacy) before moving private material into a page.

A conversation beside a page belongs to its owner. You and a colleague can work with the same page without sharing your page conversations. The page itself remains shared, so saving edits changes what the team reads.

## Private Spaces

A Space is either for the **whole workspace** or for **only its people**. Choose when you make it (the **+** beside **Spaces** in the sidebar), and change it later under the Space's **Settings** tab. A private Space has a lock beside its name.

- **What it covers:** everything in the Space: its pages and their history, page chats, search results, apps, datasets and files shared with it, and its memory.
- **Who sees it:** the people added to it, as editors or members, and workspace admins, who are editors of every Space. Everyone else doesn't see that it exists: it isn't in their sidebar, search, links, or anything a socio or connected agent tells them, and a link to it says it was not found.
- **Who makes one:** anyone. A Space you make as a member is private, with you as its editor; add the colleagues you want under **Settings**. Only workspace admins make, or open up, a Space for the whole workspace.
- **A socio can make one for you** when you ask ("make a Space called Design"). It asks first: the approval card shows the Space's name, its description and who will see it, and nothing is made until you approve it. This can't be turned off or left to automatic review. As a member you get a private Space with you as its editor; an admin can also ask for one for the whole workspace. The socio is given the new Space, so it can add pages to it right away, in your own chat with it: in a room, or a Telegram group or team topic, it can't use a private Space that the others can't see.
- **Editors** add and remove people, change their roles and make the Space private. Someone removed stops seeing it at once, their page chats about it included; added back, they see it all again.
- **Socios:** a socio uses a private Space only if it was given the Space and everyone in the chat can see it. In a room every member and everyone invited must; in Telegram groups and team topics, never.

Existing Spaces stay open to the whole workspace until an editor makes them private.

## Rename a Space

An editor of a Space (workspace admins edit every Space) can rename it from its **Settings** tab: change **Name**, then **Rename**. You can also ask a socio to rename a Space it was given, if you are one of that Space's editors. Only the name changes: its pages, chats, memory, people and who sees it stay as they are, and links to it and its pages keep working. A name is 1 to 60 characters.

## Delete a Space

An editor of a private Space can delete it from its **Settings** tab: **Delete this Space**, then type the Space's name to confirm. Only workspace admins delete a Space for the whole workspace. A deleted Space is gone for everyone at once, with its pages, the page and Ask chats about them (yours included), its shared memory, the apps, datasets and link cards shared with it, and socios' access to it. Your own apps, datasets and files stay yours. Nothing is erased: ask a workspace admin to restore it, and it comes back with everything in it.

## Give a socio the right context

A socio's settings specify its default Space and any additional Spaces it may use. Its default is where it normally creates pages from ordinary chats. In a page conversation, that page's Space is the default destination. Select a specialist that has access; if it is missing from the choices, ask its owner or an admin to review its authorized Spaces.

Access to a Space's pages is separate from access to your personal files and datasets. A link in a page does not grant a socio access to the linked personal data. A dataset shown in a page needs appropriate sharing with that Space for other readers to see its data.

## Shared memory

A Space's **Memory** tab, beside its pages, holds what the team knows together: lasting facts, conventions and decisions, one entry each ("Invoices go out on the 1st"). Socios that have the Space use the entries that match a message when they reply, and say they are the Space's shared knowledge.

- **Propose** an entry, or suggest a change to one, from the tab. A socio can propose one too when you ask it, or when the conversation settles something for the team. Nothing is used until one of the Space's **editors** approves it; a socio never approves, and nothing from your own memories or chats becomes shared without a proposal an editor approves.
- **Editors** approve proposals, in their own words if they want, reject them, edit entries and retire them. Every version is kept in the entry's history, with who proposed it, in which chat, and who approved it. Workspace admins are editors of every Space. The Space's people and their roles are under its **Settings** tab.
- **Who reads it:** whoever can see the Space: everyone in the workspace, or only its people when it is private. This is checked every time: someone removed from the Space stops getting its entries at once. In a room, socios use a Space's memory only when every member, and everyone invited, can read it; in Telegram groups and team topics they don't use it at all.

Never propose secrets or anything private about a person: approved entries go to the model provider with matching messages, as memories do.

## Keep the library useful

Use clear page titles and subpages for related material. A page can move within a Space or to another Space with its subpages. Its identity and history remain, and links to its earlier Space address can find the new location. Review any embedded dataset sharing after a move.

Continue with [editing pages](/docs/pages) and [page conversations](/docs/page-conversations). If you need a new Space for the whole workspace or a change to workspace organization, ask an admin; do not create a duplicate page just because you cannot find its current home.
